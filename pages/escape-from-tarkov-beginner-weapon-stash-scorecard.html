Escape from Tarkov beginner weapon and stash scorecard

Classify each Tarkov weapon as standardize, retain, or sell using current ammunition access, magazines, replacement burden, task fit, and stash pressure.

Reviewed 2026-09-06Evidence-bounded Tarkov guide; recheck after relevant patch changesEditorial standards

Choose a default platform that simplifies your next preparations instead of filling the stash with weapons you cannot consistently support.

Short answer

List the weapon platforms already available to you, then check each one against five inputs: repeatable ammunition and magazine access, complete replacement burden, intended near-term use, handling comfort, and stash pressure. Standardize on a platform only when those inputs support repeated use. Retain another weapon when it has a verified near-term purpose, and sell excess platforms that lack both sustainable support and a defined use. Verify current access for your active mode before committing because the supplied official evidence establishes season and patch activity without resolving specific weapon, trader, or economy details.

What this scorecard decides

Define a narrow classification tool that complements the existing weapon and stash guides.

  • Classify each candidate platform as standardize, retain for a verified near-term use, or sell.
  • Treat the result as a preparation policy rather than a weapon ranking.
  • Do not infer current weapon, ammunition, magazine, trader, task, or economy details from general patch activity.

Record the current inputs

Make every classification depend on information the player can verify for the active mode.

  • Confirm whether suitable ammunition and magazines are repeatedly accessible under the player's current conditions.
  • Estimate the burden of replacing the complete setup rather than considering the weapon alone.
  • Name the specific near-term task or intended use the platform must support.
  • Record whether the player can handle the configured platform comfortably.
  • Note whether keeping the platform prevents normal kit assembly or creates meaningful stash pressure.

Choose the classification

Turn the verified inputs into a practical stash decision.

  • Standardize when the supporting supplies are repeatable, the complete setup is replaceable within the player's limit, and the platform fits its intended use.
  • Retain for a verified near-term use when a platform is not suitable as the default but has a specific upcoming purpose and adequate support.
  • Sell excess when a platform lacks sustainable support, has no defined near-term use, and competes with more functional preparation needs.
  • Mark the decision needs review when a critical access or replacement input remains unresolved.

Resolve close decisions

Provide a conditional tie-breaker without claiming a universal weapon choice.

  • Prefer the candidate with fewer unresolved dependencies when several platforms meet the intended use.
  • Use handling comfort as a tie-breaker only after access and replacement burden are acceptable.
  • Reduce the retained quantity when storage pressure is the main constraint.
  • Avoid committing several future setups to supplies that cannot yet be replenished consistently.

Use the focused guides for deeper checks

  • Use the budget-weapon guide to examine intended range, complete kit burden, access, and handling in more detail.
  • Use the stash-triage guide to evaluate storage pressure, confirmed future uses, and replacement access.
  • Use the ammunition-and-armor assumption guide when the decision depends on target or protection assumptions.

Recheck after relevant official activity

Keep the classification patch-aware while respecting the closed evidence boundary.

  • The supplied official evidence establishes a new season and ongoing patch or hotfix activity.
  • It does not establish a specific change to the scorecard's weapon, ammunition, magazine, trader, task, or economy inputs.
  • Reverify only the dependencies used by the selected platform before changing the classification.
  • Do not describe an existing guide or prior decision as outdated without detailed authoritative evidence.

Decision checklist

  • Write down every weapon platform currently competing for stash space or preparation budget.
  • Confirm current ammunition access for each candidate in the active mode.
  • Confirm current magazine access for each candidate in the active mode.
  • Compare the burden of replacing each complete setup.
  • Assign one verified near-term purpose to every platform you intend to retain.
  • Label each candidate standardize, retain, sell, or needs review.

Worked example

A beginner compares three unnamed platforms. Platform A has ammunition and magazines the player can replenish, its complete setup fits the player's replacement limit, and the configured weapon feels manageable, so it becomes the default. Platform B has no verified near-term use and depends on supplies the player cannot currently replenish, so the excess setup is sold. Platform C has a defined upcoming use and adequate supporting supplies but is unsuitable for repeated replacement, so one setup is retained for that purpose rather than becoming the default. If any access assumption cannot be verified for the active mode, that classification remains needs review.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing from an isolated weapon statistic without checking supporting ammunition and magazines.
  • Comparing weapon prices while ignoring the complete replacement burden.
  • Keeping unsupported platforms for unspecified future plans.
  • Treating general season or patch activity as proof of a particular gameplay change.

Frequently asked questions

Which weapon should a beginner standardize on?

The closed evidence does not support naming a specific platform. Choose among your current candidates using verified ammunition and magazine access, complete replacement burden, intended use, and handling comfort.

How many copies of the default platform should I keep?

No supported fixed quantity is available. Set a limit from your near-term preparation needs, acceptable replacement burden, and available stash space.

Should I keep a valuable weapon for later?

Keep it when you can name a verified near-term use and support the required setup. Otherwise, compare that uncertain future use with current storage pressure and preparation needs.

Does recent official activity invalidate my current choice?

Not by itself. The supplied evidence establishes season and patch activity but identifies no specific change to the inputs used here. Recheck those inputs before revising the choice.
