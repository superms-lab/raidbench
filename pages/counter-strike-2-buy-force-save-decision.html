Should the team buy, force, or save this round?

Direct answer

Make one team decision from the current money, equipment already saved, utility required, likely opponent buy, and the next-round consequence. Five partial individual plans are usually weaker than one modest plan everyone can execute.

Decision signalWhat to verifyNext move
Full plan is affordableCheck rifles, armor, utility, and role coverageBuy together with a defined round plan
Only partial equipment fitsCompare force-win value with next-round damageForce only when the round has a credible conversion
Next round matters moreIdentify weapons worth carrying and exit goalsSave deliberately rather than taking isolated fights

Decision checklist

  • Call current money and saved equipment
  • Estimate the opponent's likely buy
  • Define required utility and entry roles
  • Compare this round with the next-round economy
  • Make one clear team call before purchases begin

Stop and recheck when

  • Cancel the force when the team cannot afford a coherent plan
  • Do not chase exits during a save when it risks valuable carried equipment

Common mistakes

  • Buying individually before the call
  • Counting rifles but ignoring utility
  • Forcing because the previous round felt unlucky
