Should I raid fast or use the most sulfur-efficient method?

Short answer

Choose the method with the lowest expected loss, not automatically the lowest sulfur total. A slower method is efficient only if the extra exposure does not materially raise the chance of losing boom, kits, or control of the breach. Speed is worth paying for when it protects a larger investment.

SituationFavor speedFavor sulfur efficiency
Counter pressureBusy area, visible approach, or valuable launcher exposedQuiet target with controlled sightlines and exits
Target layoutRoute must be opened before defenders can adaptShort, isolated path with no useful splash
Team capacityRoles and resupply support a high-tempo breachSmall team can hold the firing angle for longer
Resource positionLosing the raid would cost more than the sulfur premiumSulfur is the bottleneck and the position is defensible
Decision rulePay more to shorten meaningful exposureSave sulfur only when the added time is survivable

Checklist

  • Calculate the complete route with at least two viable methods.
  • Estimate how long each method leaves the team exposed and what can be lost.
  • Check whether rockets create useful splash instead of decorative noise.
  • Choose the stop condition before paying a speed premium.
  • Review the actual spend and losses after the raid to improve the next estimate.

Example

If a lower-sulfur door method saves 1,000 sulfur but keeps the shooter exposed long enough to risk a launcher and full kit, the apparent saving may be negative. On a quiet isolated target, the same method can be the better decision.

A simple expected-loss test

Write the sulfur premium for the faster route beside the additional gear and boom that the slower route exposes. If the plausible loss from extra time is larger than the premium, speed is not wasteful; it is insurance.

Common mistakes

  • Calling the lowest sulfur total the cheapest route without pricing failure.
  • Paying for speed when the route is still unscouted.
  • Assuming a large group should always use rockets.
  • Ignoring banking, sealing, and launcher recovery time.

Frequently asked questions

Is the lowest-sulfur raid method always the best?

No. Add the cost of extra exposure, likely counters, lost kits, launcher risk, and route uncertainty. The lowest item cost can have a higher expected loss.

When is faster raiding worth the sulfur premium?

Speed is valuable when it materially reduces defender response, counter exposure, or the chance of losing expensive equipment and carried boom.

How do I compare speed and efficiency without exact probabilities?

Use scenarios. Compare the faster route's sulfur premium with one plausible failure on the slower route, then state which assumption would change the choice.
