Is a 100-rocket base worth raiding in Rust?

A target-selection framework for expensive Rust bases: route confidence, likely loot, strategic value, counter exposure, reserve boom, and reasons to walk away.

Why would anyone raid a base that appears to cost 100 rockets?

Short answer

A base that appears to cost 100 rockets is not automatically worth 100 rockets, and the visible shell may not be the real route. Raid only when scouting supports a cheaper credible path, unusually strong loot or strategic value, and an exit plan that survives counters. Otherwise, walking away is the profitable decision.

EvidenceSupports raidingSupports walking away
Route confidenceA scouted door, roof, or splash route is materially cheaper than the shellThe plan assumes the core location and every hidden layer
Economic signalRepeated high-value activity and storage behavior support the estimateBase size is the only evidence of loot
Strategic valueThe base directly blocks territory, farming, or team survivalThe target is selected mainly for prestige
ExecutionTeam, reserve, sealing, banking, and exit roles are readySuccess depends on no counters and no wrong turns
Stop ruleA specific layer, reserve, or time limit ends the attemptThe only plan is to continue because boom was already spent

Checklist

  • Separate visible shell cost from the cheapest credible breach route.
  • Record direct evidence of activity and likely stored value without inventing certainty.
  • Compare resource profit and strategic value as two different verdicts.
  • Reserve enough for one named uncertainty, not every imaginable layer.
  • Walk away when the route needs several optimistic assumptions at once.

Example

A massive compound can still have a weak operational route, while a compact base can hide several expensive layers. The useful question is not how intimidating the exterior looks; it is what the best evidenced route costs and what success changes for your team.

Common mistakes

  • Treating base size as proof of stored loot.
  • Using the most expensive exterior path as the only estimate.
  • Combining resource profit and revenge into one vague justification.
  • Continuing after the breach disproves the scouting model.

Frequently asked questions

Does a large Rust base usually contain enough loot to repay the raid?

Size alone cannot establish loot value. Use observed activity, route confidence, strategic value, and the cost of failure; treat hidden loot as uncertain.

Why do groups raid apparently unprofitable bases?

Possible reasons include a cheaper hidden route, strategic pressure, territory control, threat removal, imperfect information, or a non-economic objective. None guarantees resource profit.

When should I walk away from an expensive target?

Walk away when the route, payoff, and safe exit each depend on unverified optimistic assumptions, or when the planned stop condition would be ignored after the first spend.
