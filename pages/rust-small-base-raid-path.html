Should I raid the doors or the wall of a small Rust base?

Short answer

Choose the route with the lowest risk-adjusted cost, not the fewest visible layers. Count confirmed doors, price the cheapest plausible wall or roof route, estimate where the TC and loot are likely to sit, and include exposure plus the next unknown layer before crafting.

RouteChoose it whenWarning sign
Door pathDoor count is visible and the path likely reaches the coreAirlocks, garage doors, or turns hide additional layers
Wall pathThe target room and wall grade are well identifiedHoneycomb or a false core makes the breach a side room
Roof pathRoof access is controlled and vertical layout is readableLadder hatches, traps, or poor exits create a second raid
Do not raidNone of the routes has a credible payoff or exitThe plan depends on several unverified assumptions

Checklist

  • Sketch the footprint, entrances, airlock turns, roof access, and visible upgrades.
  • Calculate a low and high estimate for each credible route.
  • Mark the likely TC, main loot room, and where a false room could sit.
  • Add exposure, splash opportunity, sealing, and escape to the comparison.
  • Choose the route only after writing what evidence would make you stop.

Example

A two-door path can look cheaper than a stone wall, but the calculation changes if the second door probably opens into another airlock while the wall is confidently identified as the core. Compare a range, not a single optimistic count.

Common mistakes

  • Counting visible doors as the complete route.
  • Assuming the most upgraded wall always protects the main loot.
  • Ignoring roof access and vertical control.
  • Continuing after the breach disproves the original layout.

Frequently asked questions

Is the door path usually cheaper than the wall path?

Sometimes, but visible doors may hide additional layers. Compare low and high route estimates and include the probability that each route reaches the TC or main loot.

Should I raid the most upgraded wall?

Not automatically. An upgraded wall can protect the core, disguise a side room, or reflect later repairs. Use the full footprint and other scouting evidence before committing.

When should I abandon the planned path?

Stop and recalculate when the breach reveals a layout that invalidates the estimated layer count, TC position, loot probability, or safe exit.
