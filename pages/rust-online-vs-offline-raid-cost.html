How much extra boom should I bring for an online raid?

Short answer

There is no honest universal buffer percentage for an online raid. Defenders can repair, seal, move loot, change the route, and contest the breach, so budget for one plausible surprise layer and define a stop condition. An offline route is more predictable, but counter-raiders and scouting errors still exist.

Planning factorOnline raidOffline raid
Route certaintyLow: defenders can alter and reseal the pathHigher, but scouting can still be wrong
Reserve boomAt least one plausible surprise layer, chosen from scoutingA smaller route-specific correction may be enough
Team jobsBreach, cover, launcher recovery, sealing, and callsBreach, counter watch, banking, and sealing
Stop conditionSet before the first explosive; defenders create sunk-cost pressureSet before the raid; counters can still erase a winning route

Checklist

  • Write the confirmed route and the most likely surprise layer.
  • Separate route boom from reserve boom so the reserve is not spent casually.
  • Assign who recovers the launcher and who carries seal materials.
  • Choose the condition that ends the raid: reserve spent, launcher lost, route invalid, or counters controlling the breach.
  • Bank valuable loot before extending into an uncertain layer.

Example

If the scouted path appears to be two garage doors, the route estimate is only the opening hypothesis. For an online attempt, reserve for the plausible next layer, assign a launcher recovery role, and stop if the reserve is spent before the expected core appears.

Common mistakes

  • Applying the same buffer percentage to every base and server.
  • Treating defender repairs and reseals as free extra layers with no stop rule.
  • Keeping all boom on one player without a recovery call.
  • Continuing only because the raid has already consumed resources.

Frequently asked questions

What percentage of extra boom should I bring to an online raid?

Use a route-specific layer, not a universal percentage. Identify the most plausible hidden or resealed layer and reserve enough for that decision; custom servers and defender behavior can change the requirement.

Are offline raids predictable?

They are usually more predictable than an active defense, but scouting errors, counters, traps, and unexpected layers still make exact-cost planning fragile.

When should an online raid stop?

Stop when a prewritten condition is met, such as exhausting the reserve, losing launcher control, discovering an invalid route, or losing control of the breach to defenders or counters.
